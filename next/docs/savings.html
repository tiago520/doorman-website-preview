Start with the comparison.

Doorman compares recorded routed cost with an estimated cost for the original-model path. The difference is a counterfactual calculation, not a promise that your provider invoice will fall by that amount.

Usage outside the gateway, missing pricing, provider contracts and applicable charges can affect the final bill.

Separate the sources.

  • Routing: a suitable cheaper model can reduce the price of eligible work.
  • Doorman-attributable cache work: eligible cache injection is tracked separately from a cache discount your client already received.
  • Other reported axes: inspect the ledger’s labels and methodology instead of assuming every reduction came from routing.

Existing provider cache savings are excluded from the Doorman-attributable headline. Keep those two numbers distinct in a business case.

Read the adjustments too.

Qualifying dissatisfaction retries can claw back an earlier saving. The canonical savings ledger also subtracts its tuned-inference surcharge; monthly-close records expose other fields such as route fees separately.

Do not describe every report as “net of every fee.” Use the definitions of the particular report you export.

Make the review repeatable.

  1. Select a consistent period and scope.
  2. Review spend, attributed savings and adjustments together.
  3. Look for missing identity, pricing and quality evidence.
  4. Compare the report with actual provider invoices and customer charges.
