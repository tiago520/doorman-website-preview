Questions your team will ask.

Will routing degrade my agents' output?

Routing is downgrade-only and decided per turn: a request moves to a cheaper model only when the classifier is confident it will do the job well; max-tier work stays on the top models. Pin a model any time — say USE OPUS and it sticks.

What happens when something fails?

The worst thing a gateway can do is show your agent an error. On any routing failure the original request is forwarded byte-identical; on upstream trouble it cascades through fallbacks and then passes through. Always-answer is the contract.

We run Zscaler or Cisco. Will it conflict?

No — Doorman doesn't fight for the endpoint in those fleets. Your secure-web-gateway forwards AI domains to the Doorman gateway, nothing is installed on laptops, and user identity arrives in a header. One inspection point per hop.

Where does my data go?

Through the gateway to your model providers — nowhere else by default. Prompts are never used for training, ZDR can be enforced per org, and every request can carry a signed residency receipt your auditor can verify independently.

How are savings computed?

Conservatively. Only what Doorman itself caused counts — model swaps, injected caching, thinking downgrades, dedup — and clawbacks plus our own fees are subtracted. The formula ships in the product, next to the number.

Can we run it ourselves?

Yes — cloud gateway in your VPC or fully on-prem and air-gapped via docker-compose. All four modes share one routing pipeline and one policy surface.
