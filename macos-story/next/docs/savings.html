Start with the comparison.

For finance and team owners: have a defined reporting window, access to that organization’s spend data, and the provider invoices or charges you will compare. Identify whether your report contains gateway-governed usage, externally imported spend or both.

Doorman compares recorded routed cost with an estimated cost for the original-model path. The difference is a counterfactual calculation, not a promise that your provider invoice will fall by that amount.

Usage outside the gateway, missing pricing, provider contracts and applicable charges can affect the final bill.

Match the report before comparing totals.

ReportScopeMeaning
Routing savings historySelected trailing periodThe cost-summary report counts routing savings for that window.
All-time savings breakdownAll recorded history in that viewIncludes the reported optimization contributions and its stated adjustments.
AI CloseThe selected UTC periodA signed period document with explicit savings, fee, quality and coverage fields.

Separate the sources.

  • Routing: a suitable cheaper model can reduce the price of eligible work.
  • Doorman-attributable cache work: eligible cache injection is tracked separately from a cache discount your client already received.
  • Other reported contributions: embedding reuse, tool compaction, eligible compression and provider-tier behavior have distinct eligibility rules. Review the optimization mechanisms ↗

Existing provider cache savings are excluded from the Doorman-attributable headline. Keep those two numbers distinct in a business case.

Read the adjustments too.

Qualifying dissatisfaction retries can claw back an earlier saving. The canonical savings ledger also subtracts its tuned-inference surcharge; monthly-close records expose other fields such as route fees separately.

A daily chart should reconcile to its own reported total within rounding. If it does not, resolve the discrepancy rather than scale the values to fit. Do not describe every report as “net of every fee.”

Make the review repeatable.

  1. Select a consistent period and scope.
  2. Review spend, attributed savings and adjustments together.
  3. Look for missing identity, pricing and quality evidence.
  4. Compare the report with actual provider invoices and customer charges.

Resolve a mismatch before publishing.

Compare organization, window, timezone, report scope, missing prices and adjustments first. External imports may add costs without proving those requests passed through Doorman. Preserve the original export and record what changed when a source is corrected. Escalate a remaining discrepancy with the period and report identifier; do not fill missing prices with zero or scale one report to another.
