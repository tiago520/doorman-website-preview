A practical walkthrough

Build a quality review you can trust.

For a support-answering workload, test grounding, required format and useful resolution before accepting a cheaper candidate.

01

Define what good means.

Choose criteria for the workload: answers supported by supplied context, required format and task completion. Include known failure cases so a high average cannot hide a critical miss.

Before you begin

Make the comparison decision-ready.

Does an evaluation change my route?

Viewing evidence does not change routing. Adoption and live evaluation are separate actions with their own permissions and costs.

Will every result have a trustworthy score?

No. Missing or uncalibrated evidence stays visible. Evaluation is a sample of the workload and depends on the quality of the criteria and human labels.

Can I connect this to release checks?

Availability depends on your gateway’s evaluation backend and configured data. The experiments workflow exposes verdicts for release checks. Confirm the configured datasets, evaluator behavior and failure policy before making a verdict a deployment gate.

What needs to exist before we run an experiment?

Confirm the evaluation backend and permissions, a representative dataset, baseline and candidate access, evaluation criteria and an owner for provider usage. Dataset and run creation availability varies by deployment; the setup guide identifies the supported path.

Put a model decision to the test.

Bring representative tasks, a baseline and the failures you cannot accept. Build a comparison your engineering team can review.
