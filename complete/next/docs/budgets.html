Set an allowance with a clear outcome.

For team administrators: decide what happens at a limit, test it on a pilot key, and give users a recovery path.

Define who and what the limit covers.

Start with an organization administrator, a verified pilot client and a budget owner. Choose the scope and the reset period before selecting a number. An organization allowance can coexist with member, department and key allowances; every applicable scope contributes to the decision.

DimensionUseful forCheck
DollarsA spend allowance over a selected period.Pricing evidence and period boundaries.
RequestsA request-count allowance, including low-cost calls.The separate count period and actual request path.
TokensAn input/output volume allowance.Reported usage and the supported token counter.
Velocity/request ceilingA sudden burst or an expensive single request.The dedicated runtime-control configuration.

Use dollar and count caps for different constraints. A monthly dollar allowance does not make a daily request cap irrelevant.

Choose the user experience at the limit.

ModeExpected behavior
monitorRecord the budget state without limiting the request.
warnAllow work and surface the configured warning.
soft_capSignal a cheaper eligible routing tier; validate its effect on your workload.
block_with_overrideStop at the limit while allowing the supported override workflow.
block_hardStop at the limit without a user override.

The most restrictive applicable outcome wins. Increasing one scope does not release a request still blocked by another. Hard limits can stop a background job; agree who responds before enabling them.

Configure one pilot scope.

  1. Open Budgets in your own organization. Choose the pilot organization, department, user or key scope.
  2. Set the dollar amount and period, then the intended enforcement behavior. Save and reread the values.
  3. Use Count caps where available to add request/token limits and their separate count period. Leave an unused dimension unset instead of treating zero as unlimited.
  4. Inspect Runtime controls for request-cost ceilings and velocity behavior. These answer different questions from a monthly allowance.

Inspect sample budgets and Count caps ↗

Example policy worksheet: “Pilot key; monthly USD allowance; daily request cap; warn first; owner reviews before enforcement.” Choose the actual amounts from your workload and acceptance criteria.

Test a warning and a refusal.

Use an expendable pilot key and non-sensitive requests. Establish a baseline, then select a deliberately small test allowance appropriate to the agreed test. Confirm the warning/refusal on the client and the matching budget state in the console. Restore the intended pilot allowance afterward.

  • Scope, reset period and enforcement match what was saved.
  • Count caps appear as requests/tokens, not mislabeled currency.
  • A count refusal identifies its exhausted dimension; inspect budget_dimension when returned.
  • A model fallback or retry still follows the intended budget behavior.
  • The owner can explain which limit applied and how work should resume.

The authenticated member budget-status route is GET /api/me/budget-status. Agent-facing MCP also exposes applicable snapshots through get_budget_status.

Review usage before widening the rollout.

Compare the actual workload with the allowance and warning cadence. Keep forecasts labeled as estimates. For long-running agents, review runtime controls and reservations alongside the period budget; a reporting task ID is not itself a separate hard spending cap.

Resolve the scope that stopped work.

Check all applicable limits and their reset times before raising a number. An authorized administrator can adjust the appropriate allowance or use the supported override path. Keep the reason and audit record. If soft-cap behavior harms a task, return to the approved policy and evaluate the route before retrying broadly.
